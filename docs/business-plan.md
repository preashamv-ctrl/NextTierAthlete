# Business Plan: NextTierAthlete
### Recruiting Websites & Social Media Management for Youth Athletes

*Originally developed under the working name "Prospect Path"; rebranded to NextTierAthlete. Content below is otherwise unchanged from the original planning session — see `docs/conversation.md` for the full history.*

---

## 1. Executive Summary

**Problem:** Parents of competitive youth athletes (ages 12–18) want to help their kids get recruited, but most families lack the time, design skill, or marketing know-how to build a professional recruiting presence. Meanwhile, college coaches increasingly evaluate prospects through digital footprint — a clean recruiting website and an active, well-curated social media presence — before ever picking up the phone.

**Solution:** NextTierAthlete builds and manages two things for each athlete client: (1) a personal recruiting website (stats, highlight film, academics, coach contacts, schedule) that can be emailed directly to college programs, and (2) ongoing social media management (Instagram/TikTok/X) that grows the athlete's visibility, packages highlight content, and builds a personal brand valuable for both recruiting and NIL opportunities.

**Target Market:** Parents of travel/club and high school athletes, starting with 1–2 high-visibility sports (recommend football and basketball, or baseball/softball, based on local density) in a single metro area, then expanding by sport and geography.

**Business Model:** A one-time website build fee plus a recurring monthly (or seasonal) social media management retainer, sold in bundled packages. Add-on revenue from highlight video editing, recruiting guidance, and partner referrals (camps, combines, recruiting services).

**The Ask / Roadmap:** This plan is built as an operator's roadmap for a bootstrapped, part-time-to-full-time launch requiring minimal starting capital (~$3,000–$6,000), not an outside-investor pitch. It can be adapted into an investor deck later if the model proves out and the founder decides to raise capital to scale faster.

*Assumption: This plan assumes a solo or small-team founder-led launch, self-funded, targeting a single U.S. metro market in year one.*

## 2. Problem & Opportunity

### Pain Points
- **Time:** Parents are already stretched thin with travel schedules, practices, and tuition/fees; building and maintaining a website or content calendar is one more job they don't have time for.
- **Skill gap:** Most families don't have web design, copywriting, or video editing skills, and off-the-shelf builders (Wix, Squarespace) still require significant time investment and design judgment.
- **Recruiting visibility:** Coaches at all levels (especially D2, D3, NAIA, and juco, which make up the vast majority of roster spots) rely heavily on unsolicited outreach and digital scouting. An athlete who is easy to find, evaluate, and contact has a real edge over one who isn't.
- **NIL awareness:** With Name, Image, and Likeness rules now extending influence down to high school athletes in a growing number of states, a well-managed social presence has value beyond recruiting — it can translate into local sponsorships and brand deals.
- **Fragmentation:** Today a family might use Hudl for film, a generic template site or none at all, and an unmanaged Instagram account — with no one tying it together into a coherent, coach-friendly presentation.

### Market Trends
*Assumption/Directional, to be validated with current data before finalizing projections:*
- Youth participation in club/travel sports has grown steadily over the past decade, particularly in baseball, basketball, volleyball, soccer, and football, driven by families investing more in year-round competitive development.
- The recruiting services and athlete marketing space (recruiting profile platforms, video/highlight tools, camps/combines) is a large and growing niche within the broader youth sports economy, which itself is commonly cited in the tens of billions of dollars annually in the U.S.
- Social media has become a default recruiting research tool for coaches, particularly at the high school-to-college transition; Instagram and Hudl are the two most commonly used platforms for prospect evaluation.
- NIL rules have expanded to include high school athletes in a majority of states, which increases the value of a clean, well-managed public profile even before college.

**Validation note:** Before finalizing marketing claims or investor materials, confirm current statistics on club sports participation, NIL state-by-state rules, and coach survey data on recruiting research habits (NCSA, Next College Student Athlete, and NFHS publish relevant surveys periodically).

## 3. Solution & Product/Service Offering

### Core Product 1: Recruiting Website
- Individual, mobile-friendly website per athlete on a shared template system (fast to build, consistent quality, low marginal cost).
- Standard sections: athlete bio, key stats/measurables, highlight video embed (from Hudl/YouTube/Instagram), season schedule, academic info (GPA, test scores, target majors), coach/reference contacts, photo gallery, and a direct-contact form for college coaches.
- Custom domain option (e.g., firstlastname.com) or branded subdomain.
- Quarterly or seasonal content refresh (new stats, new highlight clips, updated schedule).

### Core Product 2: Social Media Management
- Platform focus: Instagram and TikTok primarily (highest recruiting/NIL relevance for this age group), with X and Hudl as secondary integrations.
- Monthly content calendar: highlight clips, game-day posts, training content, achievement announcements, and personal-brand storytelling.
- Basic video editing/clipping of raw game or practice footage into postable highlights.
- Light community management: consistent posting cadence, hashtag/geo-tagging strategy, engagement prompts.
- Monthly analytics summary for parents (follower growth, engagement, reach).

### Core Product 3: Content Capture (Game & Practice Video/Photography) — Key Differentiator
Unlike most competitors, who only edit footage parents supply, NextTierAthlete can shoot original film and photography on-site. This closes the single biggest gap in a typical recruiting site (an empty or thin film section) and gives the company control over its own raw content supply chain.
- On-site filming of games and/or practices (sideline or end-zone angle depending on sport).
- Photography coverage for action shots and a real photo gallery (vs. stock/generic graphics).
- Same-week (or faster) turnaround: raw footage → clipped highlights → posted to website and social.
- Founder/operator presence at games also builds direct relationships with coaches and other parents — a built-in referral engine.

### Add-On Services (Upsell Revenue)
- Full highlight reel editing/production (longer cut for recruiting film, separate from social clips).
- Recruiting guidance add-on: checklist coaching, email templates for reaching out to college coaches, camp/combine calendar planning.
- NIL brand-readiness package: bio optimization, media kit, local sponsorship outreach templates.
- Referral placement with partner camps, combines, or third-party recruiting/video services (revenue share).
- À la carte content capture for non-retainer clients (single-game filming/photography packages).

## 5. Competitive Landscape

| Competitor Type | Examples/Category | Strength | Weakness (NextTierAthlete's opening) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recruiting profile platforms | NCSA, Next College Student Athlete, FieldLevel, SportsRecruits | Large databases, coach network claims, brand trust | Generic templated profiles, expensive packages, upsell-heavy sales process, little true social media management |
| DIY website builders | Wix, Squarespace, Carrd | Cheap, flexible | Parents must build and maintain it themselves; no sports-specific structure or ongoing management |
| Film/highlight tools | Hudl | Strong film hosting and coach adoption | Not a marketing or social growth tool; no website or brand-building |
| Freelance social media managers/video editors | Local freelancers, Fiverr/Upwork gigs | Lower cost per task | Not sports-specific, no integrated website + social package, inconsistent quality |
| DIY parent/athlete effort | Athlete or parent doing it themselves | Free | Time-consuming, inconsistent, lower production quality, easy to fall behind |

**Differentiation:**
- Bundled offer (website + social media management together) rather than a single-purpose tool.
- **Original content capture (filming + photography) in-house** — most competitors only edit footage parents already have; NextTierAthlete can supply the raw film itself, which directly solves the "empty film section" problem common to DIY and template-only sites.
- Sport-specific templates and content strategy, not a generic small-business social media package.
- Founder-led, high-touch service at a small-business price point rather than an expensive national platform's upsell funnel.
- Positioned around outcomes parents care about (visibility, coach contact, brand-building) rather than just "content."

## 6. Business Model & Pricing

### Revenue Streams
1. One-time website build fee.
2. Recurring monthly (or seasonal, e.g., 6-month) social media management retainer.
3. Add-on services (highlight editing, recruiting guidance, NIL package).
4. Referral/affiliate revenue from partner camps, combines, and recruiting-adjacent services.

### Suggested Pricing Tiers
*Assumption: pricing below is a starting hypothesis to be tested with real prospective customers, not a finalized rate card.*

| Package | One-Time Website Fee | Monthly Retainer | What's Included |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | $299 | $79/mo | Website build (template-based), 4 posts/month, quarterly content refresh |
| **Growth** | $399 | $149/mo | Website build + custom domain, 8 posts/month, highlight clip editing, monthly analytics report |

- Sibling discount (10–15% off second athlete in the same household) to increase household lifetime value.

|---|---|

## 7. Marketing & Customer Acquisition Strategy

### Channels
- **Club/travel team and high school coach relationships:** Offer a free or discounted pilot website for a well-connected team captain or standout athlete in exchange for visibility with teammates and coaches.
- **Showcases, combines, and tournaments:** Set up a booth or sponsor a local event; these are exactly where parents are already thinking about recruiting visibility.
- **Instagram/TikTok organic + paid:** Show before/after examples of athlete profiles and highlight edits — the product is inherently visual and demo-friendly.
- **Referral incentives:** Discount or free month for existing clients who refer a new paying client (teammates cluster socially, so this can compound quickly within a team or league).
- **Partnerships:** Cross-promote with private trainers, skills coaches, photographers, and camp operators who already serve this same parent audience but don't compete directly.

### Sales Funnel
1. **Awareness** — social content, event presence, coach/trainer referrals.
2. **Interest** — free "recruiting visibility audit" (quick review of the athlete's current online presence with 2–3 concrete improvement suggestions) as a low-friction lead magnet.
3. **Consideration** — sample website walkthrough, sample highlight edit, pricing tiers presented clearly.
4. **Conversion** — simple onboarding call with parent, package selection, deposit/first payment.
5. **Retention/Renewal** — renew each season/school year; expand into add-ons as the athlete advances through the recruiting timeline.

## 8. Operations Plan

### Client Onboarding Workflow
1. Discovery call with parent (and athlete if possible) — goals, sport, timeline, target schools if known.
2. Intake form/content collection — stats, existing film links, photos, academic info, social handles.
3. Website build (template-based, target 5–7 business day turnaround).
4. Parent/athlete review and revision round.
5. Social media audit and first month's content calendar built.
6. Go-live and handoff to ongoing management cadence.

### Tools/Tech Stack (Starting Point)
- **Website:** Template-based builder (e.g., Squarespace, Webflow, or a custom lightweight template system) to standardize build time.
- **Social scheduling:** A scheduling tool supporting Instagram/TikTok (e.g., Later, Buffer, or similar).
- **Video editing:** A fast mobile/desktop editor (e.g., CapCut) for clipping highlights efficiently.
- **CRM/organization:** A simple CRM or even a well-structured spreadsheet/Notion/Airtable base in year one — no need for enterprise tooling early.

### Team Structure
| Stage | Roles Needed |
|---|---|
| Launch (0–6 months) | Founder handles sales, website builds, and content — possibly one part-time contractor for video editing |
| Growth (6–18 months) | Add a part-time social media manager and/or website builder as client count grows past founder's solo capacity |
| Scale (18+ months) | Dedicated roles: sales/account management, production (websites + video), social content team; founder shifts to strategy/oversight |

### Scalability
- Standardized website templates and a repeatable content calendar structure are what allow this to scale without linear cost growth — invest early in building 2–3 clean templates per sport and a reusable monthly content framework rather than fully custom work per client.
- Batch similar tasks across clients (e.g., editing all of one week's game highlights in one sitting) to improve throughput.

## 10. Risks & Mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Seasonality (demand and content needs spike/drop with each sport's season) | Diversify across 2+ sports with staggered seasons; sell annual/multi-season packages to smooth cash flow |
| Parent budget sensitivity | Offer a lower-cost entry tier and clear, tangible before/after proof of value; avoid long contracts that create sticker shock |
| Platform/algorithm changes (Instagram/TikTok) | Diversify content distribution (website is the durable owned asset; social is the growth channel, not the only asset) |
| NCAA/NIL and state-level rule changes | Stay current on state high school NIL policy; keep NIL add-on positioning flexible and clearly compliant, avoid guaranteeing outcomes |
| Client churn after recruiting concludes (senior signs, commits) | Build renewal triggers around underclassmen siblings, off-season brand maintenance packages, and referral incentives at the moment of a signing (a natural celebration/marketing moment) |
| Competition from free/DIY tools | Lead marketing with time-saved and quality-of-output messaging, not just feature lists |
| Founder time bottleneck (single point of production failure) | Prioritize template standardization and light contractor support early, before overselling capacity |
| Scheduling conflicts (multiple clients' games at the same time) | Cap Content Capture tier client count early on; prioritize by geographic/schedule clustering; build a small bench of trusted freelance shooters before overselling this tier |
| Weather/equipment failure on game day | Basic equipment insurance and a backup camera/phone-shot fallback plan so a missed shoot doesn't mean a missed deliverable |

## 11. Milestones & Roadmap

**Phase 1 (0–6 months): Validate**
- Land first 5–10 paying clients, ideally concentrated in one or two teams/leagues for referral density.
- Refine templates, onboarding workflow, and pricing based on real client feedback.
- Track actual time-per-client to sanity-check unit economics.

**Phase 2 (6–18 months): Systematize**
- Build a lightweight contractor bench (video editor, second social manager) to remove founder bottleneck.
- Build a referral engine (formal incentive program) and expand into a second sport and/or adjacent geography.
- Introduce add-on services (NIL package, recruiting guidance) once core delivery is smooth.

**Phase 3 (18+ months): Productize/Scale**
- Consider a self-serve, lower-price software tier (athlete builds their own site on the company's template system with lighter-touch support) to reach price-sensitive customers without added labor cost.
- Evaluate licensing the playbook/template system to operators in other metro areas, or expanding directly into additional regions.
- Revisit whether outside capital would meaningfully accelerate growth once the model is proven with real unit economics.

## 14. Competitive Pricing Benchmark

*Researched via live web search, September 2026. Replaces the pure "Assumption" label on pricing (Section 12, Item C.3) with sourced market comparisons — actual willingness-to-pay with real local prospects (Appendix D.1) is still unvalidated.*

| Category | Competitor | Their Price | NextTierAthlete | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full-service recruiting platform | NCSA | $2,000–$6,000+ (upfront/financed lump sum, undisclosed until sales call) | All-Access + Capture: $499 + $249–399/mo (~$3.5–5.3k/yr, month-to-month) | Comparable total annual spend at the top tier, but far more transparent and lower commitment — no upfront lump sum |
| Recruiting profile subscription | SportsRecruits Pro | ~$600/yr (~$50/mo) | Growth: $149/mo | NextTierAthlete's mid-tier is ~3x SportsRecruits, but bundles website + posting + editing, not just a profile |
| Recruiting website (self-serve, one-time) | Prospect Pages | $400 flat | $299–499 one-time build fee | Directly in line |
| Recruiting website (self-serve, subscription) | University Athlete / Stack Athlete | $21–25/mo | *(bundled into retainer, not sold standalone)* | Different model — not directly comparable |
| Recruiting website (self-serve, subscription) | ProductiveRecruit | $25/mo athlete, $99/mo+ clubs | *(bundled)* | Different model |
| General small-business social media management | Agencies/freelancers | $500–3,000/mo; entry-level ~$750/mo for 12 posts/2 platforms | $79–399/mo for 4–8+ posts, multi-platform | **Well under market** — even the top tier is below the cheapest general agency package |
| Sports videography, per game | Beginner freelancers | $150–400/game | $75–125/game (unedited) | Below the low end |
| Sports videography, per game | Established/pro shops | $600–1,500/game | $150–225/game (filmed + edited) | Well below market |
| Recruiting highlight editing | Prodigy Highlights / ProspectReels-type services | $150–199+ per video | Included in retainer, or ~$150–225 à la carte | Comparable to below |

### Takeaways

1. **The website build fee ($299–499) is right on market** — matches Prospect Pages' $400 flat fee almost exactly, for a more full-featured, sport-specific build than DIY tools like Wix/Squarespace.
2. **The bundle is the real competitive edge, not any single line item.** No competitor sells website + social management + content capture together — replicating All-Access + Capture would mean stacking NCSA (or a $25/mo site tool) + a $750+/mo agency + a $150–1,500/game videographer. That combined-offer story, not price alone, is the strongest differentiator to lead with in sales conversations (Section 7).
3. **Social media management is priced noticeably under market** ($79–399/mo vs. $750/mo+ entry-level agency pricing). Likely intentional given parent price sensitivity and no agency overhead, but it's the line item most exposed to the risk already flagged in Section 6's unit economics: founder/contractor time is "the largest cost driver," and this is the most underpriced labor relative to market.
4. **Content Capture is the most aggressively discounted line item** ($75–225/game vs. a $150–1,500/game market range for dedicated sports videographers). Reasonable as the plan's stated "key differentiator" and a loss-leader for building the film library, but it's the tier most likely to be unprofitable per game once real travel + shoot + edit time is tracked (see Section 9's note on time cost, and Appendix D.5).

**Bottom line:** pricing sits at or below market in nearly every category, which supports conversion but increases the importance of Appendix D's validation steps — particularly D.2 (pilot with 3–5 athletes) and D.5 (time-track real deliveries) — before scaling past the pilot phase.
