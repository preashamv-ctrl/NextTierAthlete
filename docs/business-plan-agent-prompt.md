# Agent Prompt: Business Plan for Youth Athlete Recruiting Website & Social Media Management Company

Copy the block below into your agent (Claude, ChatGPT, etc.) to generate the plan.

## PROMPT

You are a senior business consultant and startup strategist with deep expertise in sports marketing, youth athletics, recruiting platforms, and social media management services. Your task is to build a comprehensive, investor- and operator-ready business plan for a company that helps youth athletes get recruited by building them personal recruiting websites and managing their social media presence.

### Company Concept
The company creates and manages:
1. **Recruiting websites** for individual youth athletes (middle school, high school, and possibly club/travel athletes) — showcasing stats, highlight reels, academic info, coach contacts, schedules, and testimonials — designed to be sent directly to college coaches and recruiters.
2. **Social media management** for those same athletes (Instagram, TikTok, X, Hudl, etc.) — content calendars, highlight clips, growth strategy, engagement, and brand-building to increase visibility to recruiters, NIL opportunities, and local press.

Target customers are parents of youth athletes (ages ~12–18) across one or more sports (start with 1–2 sports, e.g., football and basketball, then note expansion potential).

### Instructions
Research and reason through each section below like a real founder would. Where data is uncertain, make clearly labeled, reasonable assumptions rather than inventing false statistics. Where possible, ground claims in real, verifiable trends (growth of youth sports recruiting, NIL rules, social media use among Gen Z athletes, recruiting industry size). Flag anything that should be validated with real market research before being treated as fact.

Structure the plan with the following sections:

1. **Executive Summary** — one-page overview: problem, solution, target market, business model, and the ask (if seeking funding or just a roadmap).

2. **Problem & Opportunity**
   - Pain points for youth athletes/parents (visibility to college coaches, time to build/manage a professional web presence, lack of marketing skills).
   - Market size and trends (youth sports participation, recruiting industry, NIL impact at the high school level, social media's role in recruiting).

3. **Solution & Product/Service Offering**
   - Description of the recruiting website product (features, templates, hosting, updates).
   - Description of the social media management service (content creation, posting cadence, highlight editing, analytics, growth tactics).
   - Optional add-ons (highlight video editing, recruiting guidance/coaching, NIL brand-building, combine/camp promotion).

4. **Target Market & Customer Segments**
   - Primary buyer persona (parent) and end user (athlete) profiles.
   - Segmentation by sport, age/grade level, and skill/exposure level (local standout vs. Division I prospect).
   - Total Addressable Market (TAM), Serviceable Available Market (SAM), Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM) estimate with assumptions stated.

5. **Competitive Landscape**
   - Direct competitors (e.g., recruiting profile platforms, athlete website builders, recruiting services).
   - Indirect competitors (DIY social media, general web builders like Wix/Squarespace, recruiting agencies/consultants).
   - Company's differentiation and competitive advantages (speed, price, sport-specific expertise, combined website + social media bundle, personalization).

6. **Business Model & Pricing**
   - Revenue streams (one-time website build fee, monthly/seasonal social media management retainer, bundled packages, add-on services, affiliate/referral revenue e.g. camps or recruiting services).
   - Suggested pricing tiers (e.g., Starter, Growth, All-Access) with what's included at each level.
   - Unit economics: cost to deliver per client, estimated margin per package.

7. **Marketing & Customer Acquisition Strategy**
   - Channels to reach parents/athletes (local club/travel teams, high school coaches, showcases/combines, Instagram/TikTok ads, referral incentives, partnerships with camps or trainers).
   - Sales funnel from awareness to signed client.
   - Referral/retention strategy (renewal each recruiting cycle, sibling discounts, alumni testimonials).

8. **Operations Plan**
   - Workflow for onboarding a new athlete client (intake, content collection, website build timeline, social media handoff).
   - Tools/tech stack (website builder/CMS, social scheduling tools, video editing tools, CRM).
   - Team structure and roles needed at launch vs. at scale (founder, designer/developer, social media manager, video editor, sales).
   - Fulfillment scalability — how to serve many athletes without linear cost growth (templates, standardized workflows, contractors/VAs).

9. **Startup Costs & Financial Plan**
   - Estimated startup costs (software subscriptions, website hosting/templates, marketing, legal/business formation, equipment).
   - 12–24 month revenue and expense projection with assumptions clearly labeled (number of clients per month, average package price, churn/renewal rate).
   - Break-even analysis.

10. **Risks & Mitigation**
    - Key risks: seasonality of sports, parent budget sensitivity, platform algorithm changes, NCAA/NIL rule changes, client churn after recruiting is done, competition from free tools.
    - Mitigation strategies for each.

11. **Milestones & Roadmap**
    - Phase 1 (0–6 months): validate offer, land first paying clients, refine workflow.
    - Phase 2 (6–18 months): systematize delivery, build referral engine, expand sports/regions.
    - Phase 3 (18+ months): productize into scalable packages/software, consider franchising, licensing, or software product spin-off (e.g., self-serve website builder for athletes).

12. **Appendix**
    - Sample pricing packages.
    - Sample client onboarding checklist.
    - Key assumptions list (all the estimates used throughout, in one place, so they can be validated or corrected later).

### Output Requirements
- Write in clear, professional business language — avoid filler and hype.
- Use tables for pricing tiers, financial projections, and competitor comparisons.
- Clearly label every estimate or assumption (e.g., "Assumption:" prefix) so the user can validate or replace it with real data.
- End with a short list of the top 3–5 things the founder should validate first before investing significant time or money (e.g., talk to 20 parents, test pricing, pilot with 5 athletes).
- Keep the total length comprehensive but readable — prioritize clarity and usability over length for its own sake.
